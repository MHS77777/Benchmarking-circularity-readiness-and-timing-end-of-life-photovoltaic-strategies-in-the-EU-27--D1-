# Analysis details

Companion to *Will Europe's circular economy be ready for its solar waste wave? Benchmarking circularity readiness and timing end-of-life photovoltaic strategies in the EU-27 with dynamic fuzzy soft sets* (submitted to Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, special issue "Embedding Circular Practices into Renewable and Sustainable Energy Production and Management"). All numbers below are generated by the pipeline in `code/` from the data in `data/` (retrieved 20 October 2026) and are the ones reported in the manuscript.

## 1. Framework
Three layers on a common universe U = EU-27, built on the dynamic fuzzy soft set (DFSS; Saeed 2025; Saeed et al. 2026), i.e. a time-indexed family of fuzzy soft sets in which the active parameter set A_t ⊆ E itself varies with t.

| Layer | Question | Universe | Index set T | Active sets A_t | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | How circular-ready is each member state, under a monitoring framework revised in 2023? | 27 member states | windows 2012, 2016, 2020, 2023 | 11 / 11 / 11 / 18 indicators (framework in force; S2) | DFSS scores, period ranks, activation decomposition, archetypes |
| B | When does each member state's PV waste wave arrive, and how large is it? | 27 member states | years 1991–2050 | — (Weibull cohort model) | annual/cumulative EoL mass, onset, midpoint, material streams |
| C | Which circular strategy should be active, where and when? | 7 end-of-life strategies | horizons H1 2026–30, H2 2031–40, H3 2041–50 | 8 / 9 / 8 criteria (C8 enters H2; C1 leaves H3) | strategy-activation timeline per archetype |

Coupling: readiness gap G(u) = urgency(u) × (1 − readiness(u)); archetypes L (consolidated leaders, Layer-A cluster 0), EW (converging, early wave: onset ≤ 2035), LW (converging, late wave: onset > 2035).

## 2. Layer A — circularity benchmarking
**Data.** 20 country-level indicators of the Eurostat CE monitoring framework (2023 revision), with observation-status flags; 3-year windows centred on 2012/2016/2020/2023; EU-27 median imputation with confidence 0.25 for missing cells; flag-based confidence (1 / 0.90 b / 0.85 p / 0.75 e,d,s / 0.50 u,c). Flagged share rises 7.8 % → 21.5 % across windows.
**Active sets.** S2 (primary, framework-faithful): 2018 framework for 2012–2020 (food waste enters 2020), 2023 revision for 2023, subject to ≥ 22/27 reporting. S1 (robustness): any indicator with ≥ 22/27 reporting.
**Algorithm.** Phase 1 reference normalisation (benefit x/max, cost min/x); Phase 2 confidence-weighted, dimension-balanced local score P_t(u); Phase 3 equal-weight temporal mean P(u). Activation decomposition splits P_t1 − P_t0 into a value effect (t1 data on A_t0) and a structural effect (criteria entering/leaving).
**Baselines.** B1 pooled composite; B2 full-set composite with cross-window imputation; B3 listwise (9 common indicators); B4 fuzzy TOPSIS per window.
**Results (S2).** Global ranking: NL 0.681, BE 0.617, LU 0.607, FR 0.575, IT 0.563, AT 0.561, DE 0.547, EE 0.538, … BG 0.393, CY 0.391, RO 0.383, EL 0.337, Structural share of the 2020→2023 change: 0.647 (dimension-balanced weights throughout; Luxembourg −0.059 structural vs −0.003 value, period rank 3→10; Croatia +0.061 vs −0.000, 17→13; Romania +0.122; Estonia value +0.089 vs structural −0.037, 6→2). The decomposition and the score tables are produced by the same run and an assertion in `layer_a.run()` enforces it. Baseline agreement with DFSS (Kendall τb): B1 0.977, B2 0.709, B3 0.858, B4 0.789; B3 per-window agreement falls 0.93 → 0.88 → 0.82 → 0.71. Clusters: k = 2 (silhouette 0.447, bootstrap Jaccard 0.849); leaders AT, BE, DE, EE, FR, IT, LU, NL. Sensitivity over 37 specifications: median τb 0.75, min 0.61.

## 3. Layer B — PV waste wave
**Data.** Eurostat net PV capacity 1991–2024 (nrg_inf_epcrw; interior reporting gaps interpolated for DK, LU, LV, RO); population (tps00001); PV-panel WEEE collected 2016–2024 (env_waselee EE_CPV_PVP and env_waseleeos EE_LE_PVP) and PV modules put on market (t).
**Model.** IRENA/IEA-PVPS (2016) convention: F(t) = 1 − exp[−(t/30)^α], α = 2.4928 early-loss, 5.3759 regular-loss; vintage mass factor 140→42 t/MW (1990→2050); c-Si composition glass 75.5 %, polymers 10 %, Al 8 %, Si 5 %, Cu 1 %, Ag 0.05 %. Central scenario = π·early + (1−π)·regular, π = 0.18 calibrated by pooled log-least-squares on 71 country-years > 100 t (country fits: BE 0.10, CZ 0.03, DE 0.04, ES 0.00, FR 0.28, IT 0.11, NL 0.14, PL 0.89, PT 0.16). Mass check: reported put-on-market tonnage / (capacity additions × mass factor) median 0.97 (IQR 0.72–1.30, n = 107). Monte Carlo (600 draws): shape mixture, T ~ Tri(25,30,35), mass × Tri(0.85,1,1.15).
**Results.** EU-27 cumulative EoL mass (central, continued deployment): 1.4 / 7.2 / 20.9 Mt by 2030/2040/2050 (early 3.2/11.1/25.9; regular 1.0/6.4/19.8). Onset 2029 (CZ) to 2038 (IE, LV). Largest 2050 totals: DE 6.7 Mt (p10–p90 5.5–8.0), IT 2.8, ES 2.3, FR 1.7. Italy's 2022–2023 collection (21.9 / 30.0 kt) runs at the early-loss envelope (Conto Energia revamping).

## 4. Coupling — readiness gap and archetypes
Urgency = ½ (mass per capita by 2040, ratio-normalised) + ½ (onset earliness, ratio-normalised); readiness = Layer-A global score, min–max normalised. Top gaps: EL 0.68 [0.58–0.85], BG 0.49 [0.43–0.62], CY 0.42 [0.36–0.57], DE 0.38 [0.32–0.45], MT 0.38 [0.34–0.43]. Archetypes: L = DE, IT, AT, FR, EE, LU, BE, NL; EW = EL, BG, CY, MT, PT, CZ, ES, SK, DK, SI; LW = HU, RO, LT, FI, HR, SE, PL, IE, LV.

## 5. Layer C — strategy activation
**Alternatives.** a1 repair & refurbishment (R4/R5); a2 cascaded second use via repowering (R7); a3 export for second life (R7, external); a4 bulk mechanical recycling (R8 low value); a5 high-value recycling (R8 high value); a6 formalised take-back (enabling); a7 landfill/incineration (baseline).
**Criteria.** C1 regulatory readiness, C2 enforcement & compliance, C3 actor & market readiness, C4 social acceptance (institutional, from public-data proxies: WEEE collection rate, WGI regulatory quality; WGI rule of law, PV capture ratio; CE employment, CE investment, WEEE recycling rate; household WEEE collection kg/cap, municipal recycling rate); C5 net cost (EUR/t), C6 quality-weighted recovery, C7 avoided GHG, C8 CRM recovery, C9 employment (literature triangular fuzzy numbers; see `results/C_*` and workbook sheet D6).
**Institutional feasibility.** Archetype capacity κ (L / EW / LW): C1 0.55/0.41/0.50; C2 0.69/0.44/0.54; C3 0.66/0.54/0.56; C4 0.72/0.50/0.56. Feasibility f = 1 − ρ(1−κ) through a documented requirement matrix ρ(a,c) (`code/layer_c_proxies.py`, Supplement S3.1); within-archetype spread gives the fuzzy support.
**Aggregation.** Fuzzy ratio normalisation, group weights (institutional 0.40, economic 0.15, environmental 0.30, social 0.15) renormalised over A_t, graded-mean defuzzification; DFSS Phase 3 across horizons.

## 8. Revision log (Session 8, 21 October 2026)

Reviewer-style comments were addressed as follows; every change is reproducible from the code in this repository.

1. **Single Layer-A run.** `layer_a.local_scores()` previously defaulted to equal indicator weights while `dfss()` defaulted to dimension-balanced weights, so the activation decomposition (Table 5) and the worked example came from a different run than the score table (Table 4). The default is now dimension-balanced everywhere, `activation_decomposition()` takes the weighting explicitly, and `run()` asserts that the decomposition's period scores equal the DFSS period scores. The structural share is unchanged at 64.7 % ("two-thirds"); the per-state effects roughly halve (Luxembourg −0.059, rank 3→10, instead of −0.108, 1→14).
2. **Rounding.** Cumulative EU-27 arisings by 2040 are 7,249 kt → 7.2 Mt (was printed as 7.3) in abstract, highlights, results, conclusion, cover letter and graphical abstract.
3. **Readiness-gap bands.** Only Greece's p10–p90 band is separated from all others; Bulgaria's and Cyprus's overlap each other and those of DE, MT, PT, ES, HU. Text now says so.
4. **Export for second life** ranks 4th for the late-wave archetype in 2031–2040 and 4th globally; "competitive only in 2026–2030" replaced by "mid-table throughout, strongest before 2030, never dominant".
5. **Onset definition** unified: year in which cumulative arisings from the stock installed by 2024 reach 10 % of that stock's cumulative-to-2050 arisings (Methods, Table 7, Fig. 8 caption and title).
6. **Germany calibration statement**: within 11 % in 2019–2020, +32 % in 2021, +60 % in 2022 (was "within 25 % through 2021"). WEEE scope labels corrected (Annex I ten-category scope 2016–2018; Annex III six-category open scope 2019–2024). Fig. 6 now plots all 128 country-years (HU 2018 hollow) and states 71 in the fit.
7. **Table 9** now applies the archetype cost multipliers (0.90/1.00/1.15) exactly as `layer_c.decision_matrix()` does; EU-27 row unscaled.
8. **Table 8** sorted by rank (SE/HR were swapped); the dangling "Table S3" cross-reference now points to Supplement S2.4; WEEE, IRENA/IEA-PVPS, TOPSIS, CRM and WGI defined at first use.
9. **Symbols.** Alternatives are a_1…a_7 (lower case; data files use a1…a7), the Weibull scale is η (not T, which is the period index), Spearman's coefficient is r_s (ρ is the requirement matrix), and the archetypes are L / EW / LW (CE is reserved for circular economy).
10. **Literature.** Added and discussed Kastanaki (2025, RCR; 2026, Renewable Energy), Pitacco et al. (2026, JCP), Nagarajan et al. (2026, SEMSC), D'Adamo et al. (2023, WM&R; 2024, SPC) and Martínez Moreno et al. (2023, JCP); withdrew the "first calibration" claim and the Table 1 entries implying no earlier validation against collection data; the contribution is now stated as the integration of structural-break benchmarking, calibrated waste wave and horizon-specific strategy choice.
